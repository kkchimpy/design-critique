# 5 — Fogg Behavior Model (B = MAP)

**Use when:** the design's job is to make the user *act* — sign up, convert,
onboard, complete a task, adopt a habit. Diagnoses why a target behavior isn't
happening.
**Source:** BJ Fogg, Stanford Behavior Design Lab.
**Related:** pairs with cognitive biases ([10](10-cognitive-biases.md)) for
persuasion and Nielsen for ability/friction. See [INDEX.md](INDEX.md).

## The model

**B = MAP** — a behavior (B) happens when **Motivation**, **Ability**, and a
**Prompt** converge at the same moment. If the behavior doesn't occur, at least one
is missing.

1. **Motivation** — the user's desire to do the behavior. Core motivators:
   sensation (pleasure/pain), anticipation (hope/fear), belonging
   (acceptance/rejection). Motivation is unreliable and expensive to raise.
2. **Ability** — how easy the behavior is to do. Increase ability by making it
   *simpler* across six factors: time, money, physical effort, mental effort
   (brain cycles), social deviance, and non-routine. **Simplicity > motivation** —
   making something easier is usually cheaper than motivating harder.
3. **Prompt** — the cue/trigger to act *now*. Three types:
   - *Spark:* prompt that also boosts motivation (for motivated-but-unable users).
   - *Facilitator:* prompt that also makes it easier (for able-but-unmotivated... 
     actually for motivated-but-low-ability users).
   - *Signal:* a simple reminder (for users high in both motivation and ability).

A prompt only works when motivation and ability are already high enough — i.e., the
user is above the **action line**.

## Evaluation questions

- What is the single target behavior this screen wants? Is it obvious?
- Is the primary action the easiest thing to do on the screen (visual prominence,
  one tap, sensible defaults)?
- Which of the six simplicity factors is the biggest barrier here?
- Is there a clear, well-timed prompt (CTA), and is it the right type for this user's
  likely motivation/ability?
- Is the design trying to *raise motivation* when it could just *reduce effort*?

## Gotchas

- Designers reflexively try to boost motivation (more copy, more urgency) when
  increasing **ability** (fewer fields, smarter defaults) is more reliable.
- A beautiful CTA fails if it fires when the user is below the action line — timing
  of the prompt matters as much as its design.
- "Ability" is not just physical ease; *mental effort* and *non-routine* are the most
  commonly overlooked barriers on first-run screens.
